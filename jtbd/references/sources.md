# JTBD Sources

Annotated bibliography for the JTBD skill — what to recommend or cite, and why.
Ordered for use: quick starts by role, then the core books, the primary literature, live sources, then adjacent reading.

## Start here (one line each, by role)

- Product/UX — *The Jobs To Be Done Playbook* (Kalbach), then Ulwick's *Jobs to Be Done: Theory to Practice*.
- Sales/founders — *Demand-Side Sales 101* (Moesta), then *Competing Against Luck* (Christensen).
- Theory depth — *When Coffee and Kale Compete* (Klement).

## Core books

The seven books worth owning outright, not just skimming a summary of.

- **Competing Against Luck** — Clayton Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon, David S. Duncan (2016)
  The jobs-as-progress theory from its namer.
  The milkshake study, the hiring/firing-a-product frame, and how experiences and integration form around the job.

- **Jobs to Be Done: Theory to Practice** — Anthony W. Ulwick (2016)
  The Outcome-Driven Innovation manual: job maps, desired outcomes, opportunity scoring, outcome-based segmentation.
  Free PDF/audiobook: https://jobs-to-be-done-book.com/

- **Demand-Side Sales 101** — Bob Moesta with Greg Engle (2020)
  Selling reframed as helping someone make progress, not persuading them.
  The six-stage timeline and four forces applied to real deals — the definitive JTBD-for-sales text.

- **The Jobs To Be Done Playbook** — Jim Kalbach (2020)
  The best practitioner synthesis of both schools; concrete techniques and plays for using JTBD inside a product org.
  Source of the neutral term "job performer."

- **When Coffee and Kale Compete** — Alan Klement (2018)
  The sharpest statement of jobs-as-progress theory, with a full systems view of what "progress" means.
  Free to read online.

- **Intercom on Jobs-to-be-Done** — Intercom (Des Traynor et al.)
  Short free ebook on how a real product org adopted JTBD.
  The job story format's home turf.

- **The Innovator's Solution**, ch. 3 — Clayton Christensen & Michael Raynor (2003)
  Where jobs-to-be-done first reached a mass business audience, years before it had a book of its own.

## Key articles & papers

Shorter primary sources — cite these for a specific claim instead of pointing at an entire book.

- "Know Your Customers' 'Jobs to Be Done'" — Christensen, Hall, Dillon, Duncan, *Harvard Business Review*, 2016
  The canonical short introduction; read before the book if time is short.

- "Turn Customer Input into Innovation" — Anthony Ulwick, *Harvard Business Review*, 2002
  First mainstream statement of outcome-driven thinking, well over a decade before Ulwick's own ODI book.

- "The Customer-Centered Innovation Map" — Lance Bettencourt & Anthony Ulwick, *Harvard Business Review*, 2008
  The job-mapping method paper itself: the 8-step Universal Job Map, worked in detail.

- "Replacing The User Story With The Job Story" — Alan Klement, 2013
  Origin article for the job story format.
  https://jtbd.info/replacing-the-user-story-with-the-job-story-af31c0f513c8 (companion: "5 Tips For Writing A Job Story," same publication)

- "The Jobs-to-be-Done Language of Innovation" — Tony Ulwick
  The complete ODI glossary, useful as a terminology check: https://jobs-to-be-done.com/the-jobs-to-be-done-language-of-innovation-c5c9b69685ef

- "The 6 Stages of Making a Purchase" — Bob Moesta
  https://medium.com/demand-side-sales/the-6-stages-of-making-a-purchase-3fdba5322fc3
  Canonical timeline description also at https://therewiredgroup.com/learn/the-six-stages-of-the-buyers-timeline/

- The milkshake story on video — Clay Christensen's talk
  Widely available by searching "Christensen milkshake." Worth watching for the reasoning style, not just the anecdote.

## Sites & ongoing

Where the frameworks keep developing outside of print.

- `strategyn.com` — Ulwick's firm; ODI process material, white papers, case studies.
- `jobs-to-be-done.com` — Ulwick's publication on Medium; templates and worked examples.
- `jtbd.info` — Klement's publication; the progress-school essays.
- `therewiredgroup.com/learn` — Moesta/Spiek applied material; switch-interview technique and demand-side sales resources.
- Christensen Institute (`christenseninstitute.org`) — continuing jobs theory work out of Christensen's research institute.
- *Circuit Breaker* — Bob Moesta & Greg Engle's podcast; live JTBD thinking applied to real product and sales problems. Earlier incarnation: *Jobs-to-be-Done Radio* with Chris Spiek.

## Sidelights

Adjacent reading — not core JTBD texts, but useful for context or for testing how far the framework travels.

- **Learning to Build** — Bob Moesta (2021)
  The demand-side skills underneath the method itself.

- **Job Moves** — Ethan Bernstein, Michael B. Horn, Bob Moesta (2024)
  JTBD applied to career changes rather than products; useful for seeing the framework travel outside product/sales.

- "The Jobs to be Done Framework as a Method to Understand Demand" — Commoncog
  https://commoncog.com/jobs-to-be-done-understand-demand/ — a clear-eyed practitioner's account of the two schools and what each is actually for.

## How to cite the schools

Progress-school claims — jobs as causal forces of progress, struggling moments, switch triggers, the four forces — trace to Christensen, Moesta, and Klement.
Activities-school mechanics — job maps, desired outcome statements, the opportunity algorithm, outcome-based segmentation — trace to Ulwick and Strategyn.
The two schools don't fully agree on what "the job" even is: a single causal statement, or a decomposable map of sub-jobs and outcomes.
When they disagree, cite the specific school and say so — don't blend them into a fictional consensus.
