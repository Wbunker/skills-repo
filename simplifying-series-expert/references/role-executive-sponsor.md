# Role: The Executive Sponsor

Load when advising the person who **funds and authorizes** an initiative and answers for its return and risk. Their lever is *money and mandate*, applied in stages. Pair with `slicing-techniques.md` if they need to actually cut the stages, and `core-concepts.md` for the underlying reasoning.

## What the Sponsor Cares About
Return on investment, downside risk, time-to-value, and not being trapped in a sunk-cost death march. The sponsor's enemy is the **big-bang bet**: a large sum committed before any decision is validated, where one early wrong assumption sinks the whole outlay.

## The Reframe for Sponsors
Stop funding *projects* (one lump, one delivery, one verdict at the end). Fund a **sequence of growth stages**, each of which returns risk reduction, value, income, a market learning point, and the option to pivot. Funding becomes a series of **real options**, not a single irreversible bet.

> The more decisions stacked up before feedback, the slower and riskier the organization moves. The sponsor's job is to stop authorizing large stacks of unvalidated decisions.

## Core Moves
- **Stage-gate the money to the slices.** Release funding per growth stage, not per annual budget. Each release of funds buys the next slice *and* the information it produces.
- **Demand "pay to learn" first.** Insist the first funded slices attack the biggest uncertainties (Will anyone buy? Does the tech integrate? Is the regulation passable?). Buying that information early is cheap insurance.
- **Make stopping respectable.** Pre-commit that **trimming the tail** — choosing not to fund the low-value remainder — is a success, not a failure. Set, up front, the conditions under which you'd stop or pivot.
- **Judge by working stages, not by reports.** Progress = released, value-earning slices in the real world, not phase completion or % of spec built.
- **Protect optionality.** Favor plans that keep pivot/stop cheap. Be suspicious of architectures or contracts that only pay off if *everything* is built.

## Questions a Sponsor Should Ask the Team
- "What is the thinnest version we could put in front of real users/customers, and when?"
- "Which decision does the next funded slice validate?"
- "What would we see that tells us to pivot — or to stop?"
- "What is the tail we are planning *not* to build?"
- "How soon does this start earning or teaching?"

## Sponsor Anti-Patterns
- Approving one large budget for one delivery a year out ("we'll see results at launch").
- Treating a stop/pivot decision as a political failure, so the team marches on past the point of value.
- Measuring progress by spend or by phase sign-offs instead of by released value.
- Funding the easy, certain work first because it shows "progress," leaving the real risks unvalidated until late.

## What Good Looks Like
Money flows in small increments tied to growth stages; the riskiest questions are answered first and cheaply; value and income start early and fund the rest; and there's a standing, blame-free option to pivot or trim the tail the moment the returns stop justifying the spend.
