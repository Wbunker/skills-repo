# Role: The Business Person

Load when advising the **business owner / entrepreneur / business-line leader** — the person growing a business or business initiative, not managing a software project. Their lever is *which bet to place in the market next*. Pair with `slicing-techniques.md` to cut the growth stages; `core-concepts.md` for the why.

## What the Business Person Cares About
Customers, revenue, market fit, and not pouring a year of cash into something the market doesn't want. Their version of big-bang is the **grand launch**: build the whole offering in secret, launch big, and find out at the worst possible moment whether anyone wants it.

## The Reframe
A business initiative is an elephant to be sliced into **15–20 growth stages**. Each stage is a real move in the market that earns a little, teaches a lot, and keeps the cost of changing course small. You **grow the business the way you grow the solution** — slice, release, learn, adjust.

## Core Moves
- **Sell the smallest real thing first.** The first growth stage should put a genuine offer in front of a real customer for real money (even hand-made, even manual behind the scenes). Money changing hands is the truest market signal — it's how you *pay to learn* what people actually want.
- **Each release earns and teaches.** Sequence stages so revenue and market learning arrive as early as possible; let early income fund later stages.
- **Pivot cheaply.** Because little is built on untested assumptions, you can change direction "with the smallest energy." Treat each stage as a checkpoint: double down, adjust, or pivot.
- **Manual before automated.** Deliver value by hand first (concierge style); automate only the parts the market has proven worth automating. Don't build infrastructure for demand you haven't confirmed.
- **Trim the tail of the offering.** Many planned features/products will turn out not to be worth building once customers reveal what they value. Not building them is profit, not failure.

## Questions a Business Person Should Ask
- "What's the smallest offer a real customer would pay for this week?"
- "What will this stage teach me about the market?"
- "If the response is lukewarm, what's my pivot — and how cheap is it right now?"
- "Which planned parts am I prepared to never build?"
- "Is this stage adding new capability (incremental) or polishing what exists (iterative)? Am I sure which I need next?"

## Anti-Patterns
- **Stealth grand launch:** months of building with no market contact, betting everything on launch day.
- **Build-it-and-they-will-come:** automating and scaling before a single customer has paid.
- **Feature maximalism:** treating the full product vision as mandatory instead of as a menu to trim.
- **Confusing motion with traction:** counting features shipped instead of value/income earned and lessons learned.

## What Good Looks Like
The business grows in visible market stages: a tiny paid offer first, each subsequent release earning a bit more and teaching something specific, course-corrections made early and cheaply, and a deliberate decision to stop expanding once additional stages stop paying for themselves — more value from the same effort.
