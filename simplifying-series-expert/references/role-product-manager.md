# Role: The Product Manager

Load when advising the person who owns **what gets built and in what order** — roadmap, backlog, prioritization. Their lever is *sequence and scope*. Pair with `slicing-techniques.md` (the PM is the heaviest user of it) and `core-concepts.md` for incremental-vs-iterative discipline.

## What the Product Manager Cares About
Delivering outcomes, validating assumptions about users, and spending build capacity where it returns the most value and learning. Their big-bang trap is the **fat release**: a giant themed milestone that bundles many unvalidated assumptions and ships them all at once.

## The Reframe
A roadmap is not a list of big features with dates — it's a **sequence of thin, end-to-end, releasable slices**, ordered by *value plus learning*, each one validating the smallest possible set of decisions. The backlog is a hypothesis queue, and the PM's craft is slicing thin and sequencing well.

## Core Moves
- **Slice every epic vertically.** Refuse layer-shaped or "phase 1/2/3" stories. Each backlog item should be demoable and releasable on its own. Use the slicing patterns (workflow step, rule, data variation, happy-path-first, zero/one/many, etc.) in `slicing-techniques.md`.
- **Prioritize by value *and* uncertainty.** Pull risky, assumption-heavy slices forward to *pay to learn* early; don't let the backlog front-load only the safe, certain work.
- **Sequence to grow value.** Order the middle of the backlog so usable value and learning land as early and as often as possible; each release updates the roadmap.
- **Name and trim the tail.** Most backlogs have a long low-value tail. The PM's high-leverage act is deciding what *not* to build. Make "trimmed" a real backlog state.
- **Separate incremental from iterative items.** Distinguish "add a new capability" (increment) from "improve/rework an existing one" (iteration) in the backlog — they're prioritized and estimated differently, and stakeholders need to know which they're getting.
- **Define MVP as the thinnest learning/earning slice**, not a stripped-down everything. If it doesn't produce a learning point or value, it isn't minimal *or* viable — slice again.

## Questions a PM Should Ask
- "What's the thinnest end-to-end version of this we could ship and learn from?"
- "Which user/market assumption does this slice test?"
- "Is this item new capability or rework? Does the roadmap make that clear?"
- "What's in the tail we're choosing not to build this cycle?"
- "If we shipped only the top 3 slices, how much of the value would we already have?"

## Anti-Patterns
- **Layered or phased stories** masquerading as increments.
- **Roadmap-as-feature-list with dates**, committing scope before learning.
- **Validating the safe parts first**, deferring the risky assumptions to the end.
- **Backlog hoarding:** treating every item as someday-mandatory; never trimming.
- **Polish creep:** iterating endlessly on an existing feature (iterative) while no new capability (incremental) ships — or vice versa.

## What Good Looks Like
A roadmap of thin vertical slices ordered to retire risk early and harvest value continuously; a backlog where incremental and iterative items are distinguished; a visibly trimmed tail; and an MVP defined as the smallest slice that earns or teaches — producing more outcome from the same delivery capacity.
